# Data protection notice (ratings study)

**Controller.** Nathan Soussan, acting as an independent researcher (nsoussan0@gmail.com).

**Purpose and legal basis.** Research on online ratings and local competition in the French hearing-aid market (working paper *Ratings Without Exit*, 2026, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22286005). The processing rests on the controller's legitimate interest in conducting and publishing non-commercial scientific research (GDPR, Article 6(1)(f)), with the safeguards for research of Article 89(1): data minimisation, no decision or measure concerning any individual, aggregated reporting only.

**Sources and categories of data.** (1) RPPS public extraction (Agence du Numérique en Santé, file *Personne_activite*, August 2026, Licence Ouverte 2.0): structure identifier, legal and trade names, professional address and telephone number of each site, and for each practitioner the registration identifier, name, mode of practice and role. (2) Google Places API (New), official access, 3 September 2026: for a sample of 2,999 sites and for 2,574 hairdressing salons, the listing's display name, address, coordinates, rating, number of reviews, website, business status and type; no review text and no data about reviewers. (3) Publicly accessible provider websites, read on 3 and 4 September 2026 (page text, which may contain the practitioner's name). (4) Commune-level statistics from the companion accessibility study.

**Processing.** Practitioner names were used only to classify sites by brand through the legal name and were not otherwise processed. Google fields and website codes were linked to sites in a working file held by the controller for the analyses of the paper. Practitioner names appearing in website text were not extracted or used.

**What is published.** `sites_public_v2.csv`: one row per RPPS structure (8,481), keyed by the register's own structure identifier so that the layer can be rebuilt and checked against the public extraction. Because that identifier is public, every row can be linked back to the register; for the 6,274 structures with a single registered practitioner, the row therefore describes that practitioner's professional activity, and the file is personal data within the meaning of Article 4(1) GDPR for those rows. The variables published are limited to what is already in the register (organisational form, brand for chains and networks, the practice-holder flag, number of practitioners top-coded at 4), to statistics computed from public geography (competition, population and demand within 10, 20 and 30 km, accessibility index), to the sampling design (stratum and weight), and to binary codes of what the provider's own public website displays. No name, registration number, mode of practice, street address, telephone number or Google field is included, and nothing in the file reveals information about a practitioner that is not already public.

**What is not published.** Site-level Google data (rating, review count, website address, place identifier), raw website text, the legal and trade names, addresses and telephone numbers of sites, the list of practitioners at each site, and the working files that carry them. These working files were versioned in the repository before 3 September 2026 and remain in the commit history of that period; they contain only fields of the public register.

**Recipients.** The published file and the code are hosted on GitHub and archived on Zenodo, and are therefore accessible to anyone.

**Retention.** Register extraction: retained as downloaded, for reproducibility. Google fields: place identifiers, which the platform terms allow to be stored, are retained; all other Google fields are deleted from the working files when the paper reaches its final version and in any case no later than 3 September 2027. Raw website text (both readings): deleted no later than 3 September 2027. Working file linking sites, Google fields and codes: reduced at the same date to the published layer and the aggregated tables.

**Rights.** Any practitioner registered at a site in the layer may ask the controller for access to, rectification or erasure of, or restriction of, the data concerning them, and may object to the processing on grounds relating to their particular situation (Article 21). An objection leads to the removal of the corresponding row from the published file and from the working file, without effect on the aggregated results already published. Complaints may be lodged with the CNIL (www.cnil.fr).

**Why the persons concerned are not informed individually.** Informing individually the several thousand practitioners listed in the register would involve a disproportionate effort for a research processing that uses only public data (Article 14(5)(b)); this notice, published with the data and the paper, is the measure taken instead.

**Platform terms.** Google Maps Platform content is collected through the official API and used only in aggregated form; it is not redistributed. The website reading skipped domains whose robots.txt disallowed all automated access; path-level rules were not evaluated.
